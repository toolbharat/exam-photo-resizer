What is a Recurring Deposit (RD) and Why Should You Invest?

A Recurring Deposit (RD) is a popular savings scheme offered by banks and post offices in India. It is designed for individuals who want to build a savings corpus through regular, disciplined monthly contributions. Unlike a Fixed Deposit (FD) where you invest a lump sum amount once, an RD allows you to deposit a fixed amount every month for a predetermined tenure. This makes it an ideal choice for salaried individuals and those who want to cultivate a habit of saving. Our RD Calculator helps you estimate the maturity amount and the interest you will earn over your chosen tenure.

How Does an RD Work?

When you open an RD account, you commit to depositing a specific amount (e.g., ₹5,000) every month for a fixed period (e.g., 24 months). The bank pools these monthly deposits and pays you interest on the accumulated amount. The interest rate for RDs is usually the same as that for FDs of similar tenure. Interest is compounded quarterly, which means your returns start earning returns themselves. At the end of the tenure, you receive the total principal plus the interest earned.

Key Features of RD

  • Tenure: RDs are typically available for tenures ranging from 6 months to 10 years.
  • Minimum Deposit: You can start an RD with as little as ₹100 per month, depending on the bank.
  • Interest Rate: Usually in the range of 6.5% to 7.5%, similar to FD rates. Senior citizens often get an additional 0.5% interest.
  • Compounding: Most banks compound interest quarterly.
  • Premature Withdrawal: Allowed, but usually with a penalty (0.5% to 1% reduction in interest rate).

RD vs FD: Which is Better?

The choice between an RD and an FD depends on your financial situation:

  • FD (Fixed Deposit): Choose an FD if you have a lump sum amount (like a bonus, inheritance, or maturity proceeds from another investment) that you want to invest at once.
  • RD (Recurring Deposit): Choose an RD if you want to save from your monthly income (salary). It instills financial discipline and helps you build a corpus over time.

Both instruments are safe, offer guaranteed returns, and come with tax benefits under Section 80C (Old Regime) if the tenure is 5 years or more.

Taxation on RD Interest

The interest earned on an RD is fully taxable as per your income tax slab. If the total interest earned from all RDs and FDs with a single bank exceeds ₹40,000 in a financial year (₹50,000 for senior citizens), the bank will deduct 10% TDS (Tax Deducted at Source). You can avoid TDS by submitting Form 15G (for non-seniors) or Form 15H (for seniors) if your total income is below the taxable limit.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q: Can I withdraw my RD prematurely?
A: Yes, premature withdrawal is allowed. However, the bank may charge a penalty by reducing the applicable interest rate by 0.5% to 1%.

Q: Is it possible to take a loan against my RD?
A: Yes, most banks offer loans of up to 80-90% of the RD balance. The RD continues to earn interest while you use the loan.

Q: What happens if I miss a monthly deposit?
A: Missing an installment may attract a penalty fee. If you miss multiple deposits, the bank may close the account and refund the balance with applicable interest.

Q: Can I open an RD for a minor?
A: Yes, parents or guardians can open an RD account on behalf of a minor. It's a great way to save for your child's future.
