What is HRA and How to Calculate Tax Exemption?

House Rent Allowance (HRA) is a crucial component of your salary structure, specifically designed to help employees manage their rental expenses. For millions of salaried professionals living in rented apartments, HRA is not just an allowance; it's a significant tax-saving tool. Under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act, a portion of the HRA you receive can be exempted from your taxable income, reducing your overall tax liability.

However, calculating the exact exemption amount can be tricky because it depends on several factors: your salary, the rent you pay, and the city you live in. Our HRA Exemption Calculator simplifies this by applying the official rules to give you the precise exempt and taxable HRA amounts.

How is HRA Exemption Calculated?

The Income Tax Department mandates that the HRA exemption is the minimum of the following three amounts:

  1. Actual HRA Received: The total HRA component in your salary.
  2. Rent Paid minus 10% of Basic Salary: (Total Rent Paid) - (10% of Basic + DA).
  3. 50% or 40% of Basic Salary: 50% for metro cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) and 40% for non-metro cities.

Example of HRA Calculation

Let's say you live in Mumbai (Metro) and your monthly details are:

  • Basic Salary + DA = ₹50,000
  • HRA Received = ₹20,000
  • Rent Paid = ₹25,000

Now, let's calculate the three conditions:

  1. Actual HRA received = ₹20,000
  2. Rent paid - 10% of Basic+DA = 25,000 - (10% of 50,000) = 25,000 - 5,000 = ₹20,000
  3. 50% of Basic+DA (Metro) = 50% of 50,000 = ₹25,000

The minimum of these three is ₹20,000. So, your exempt HRA per month is ₹20,000, and the remaining ₹0 is taxable.

Key Conditions for Claiming HRA

  • HRA exemption is only available under the Old Tax Regime. If you opt for the New Tax Regime, you cannot claim HRA exemption.
  • You must actually be living in a rented house and paying rent.
  • You cannot claim HRA if you own the house you live in.
  • If you pay more than ₹1 lakh as rent in a financial year, you must provide the landlord's PAN card to your employer.
  • If you live with your parents, you can pay them rent and claim HRA, provided they declare it as income and you have a valid rent agreement.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q: Can I claim both HRA and home loan tax benefits?
A: Yes, you can claim both if you live in a rented house (due to job location) while your own house is let out or under construction. The rules have specific conditions for this scenario.

Q: What if my salary structure doesn't include HRA?
A: If your employer does not provide HRA, you cannot claim HRA exemption under Section 10(13A). However, you might be able to claim a deduction under Section 80GG (up to ₹60,000 per year) if you meet specific criteria.

Q: How is 10% of Basic Salary calculated for HRA?
A: It is calculated on (Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance) that is specifically considered for retirement benefits. Most commonly, it's just the Basic Salary.

Q: Can I submit a rent receipt to my employer?
A: Yes, you must submit rent receipts to your employer. If the annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh, PAN details of the landlord are mandatory.
