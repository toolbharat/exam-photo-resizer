Understanding Gratuity: A Complete Guide

Gratuity is a statutory benefit provided by an employer to an employee as a token of appreciation for their long-term service. It is governed by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, and applies to companies with ten or more employees. Whether you are retiring, resigning, or being laid off, if you meet the eligibility criteria, you are entitled to receive this lump sum amount. Calculating the exact amount manually can be confusing, but our Gratuity Calculator makes it simple and accurate.

This guide will walk you through what gratuity is, how it is calculated, tax implications, and common questions employees have.

What is Gratuity?

Gratuity is a financial reward given by an employer for an employee's dedicated service over several years. It is essentially a retirement benefit, but it also acts as a safety net for employees who resign after a long tenure. The amount received can be used for any purpose—starting a business, funding a child's education, or simply saving for the future.

Eligibility Criteria for Gratuity

To qualify for gratuity, you must meet the following conditions:

  • You must have completed at least 5 years of continuous service with the same employer.
  • The payment is due upon retirement, resignation, superannuation, or death/disablement.
  • The 5-year rule is waived in case of death or permanent disability.
  • You must be covered under the Payment of Gratuity Act (organizations with 10+ employees).

How is Gratuity Calculated?

The formula for gratuity calculation depends on whether the employer is covered under the Gratuity Act. For covered employers, the formula is:

Gratuity = (15 × Last Drawn Salary × Years of Service) / 26

Where:
15 = 15 days' salary for each completed year
Last Drawn Salary = Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance (DA)
26 = Number of working days in a month (standard)

For employees not covered under the Act, the formula is slightly different: Gratuity = (15 × Last Drawn Salary × Years of Service) / 30. However, our calculator uses the standard formula applicable to most Indian employees.

Examples of Gratuity Calculation

Example 1: Suppose your last drawn salary (Basic + DA) is ₹50,000 per month, and you have worked for 10 years.
Gratuity = (15 × 50,000 × 10) / 26 = ₹2,88,461 (approx).

Example 2: If your salary is ₹30,000 and you have served for 7.5 years, the last 6 months are rounded up to 8 years.
Gratuity = (15 × 30,000 × 8) / 26 = ₹1,38,461 (approx).

Example 3: For a high earner with a salary of ₹1,00,000 and 20 years of service:
Gratuity = (15 × 1,00,000 × 20) / 26 = ₹11,53,846 (approx).

Taxation on Gratuity

Gratuity is one of the most tax-friendly components of your salary. The good news is:

  • Gratuity up to ₹20 lakh is completely tax-exempt for employees covered under the Gratuity Act.
  • Any amount above ₹20 lakh is considered taxable income and is added to your total income for that financial year.
  • This ₹20 lakh exemption limit is a lifetime limit per employee.

This high exemption limit makes gratuity an excellent retirement corpus builder. For many employees, the entire gratuity amount is tax-free.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q: Is gratuity mandatory for all companies?
A: It is mandatory for companies with 10 or more employees. Smaller companies may offer it voluntarily, but it is not legally required.

Q: Can I claim gratuity if I resign before 5 years?
A: No, you must complete 5 years of continuous service. However, exceptions apply in case of death or disability.

Q: How is "continuous service" defined?
A: Continuous service includes all years of work without a break. If you take unpaid leave or have gaps, it might affect eligibility. However, paid leave, maternity leave, and layoffs do not break continuous service.

Q: What if my employer refuses to pay gratuity?
A: You can file a complaint with the Labour Commissioner. The employer is legally obligated to pay if you meet the criteria.

Q: How is gratuity paid?
A: It is usually paid as a lump sum after you leave the organization, usually within 30 days of the final settlement.
