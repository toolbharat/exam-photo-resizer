Why Do PDFs Have Passwords?

PDF passwords are used for two main reasons:

  • Security: To prevent unauthorized people from opening the file (User Password).
  • Restrictions: To prevent editing, copying, or printing the file (Owner Password).

If you have the password, you can unlock the file. Our tool helps you remove both types of passwords easily.

What If You Don't Know the Password?

Our tool cannot guess or crack a password if you don't know it. It is designed for people who own the file or have the password but want to remove it for convenience (e.g., to merge it with other files or print it).

If you have forgotten the password, you will need to contact the person or organization that created the PDF. There are no safe ways to crack a PDF password.

Other Ways to Unlock a PDF

If you don't want to use our online tool, here are some alternatives:

  • Adobe Acrobat Reader (Desktop): Open the PDF with the password, go to "File" > "Protect" > "Remove Security".
  • Google Chrome: Open the PDF with the password, click the "Print" icon, and choose "Save as PDF". This will create a new unlocked PDF (but might reduce quality).
  • Mac Preview: Open the PDF with the password, go to "File" > "Export" and save it as a new PDF without a password.
